Perfect Ribeye Steaks
Great on the grill

Serves: 2 Course: Main Type: Meat
Time: 30 Min Level: Easy For two

Ingredients

2 ribeye steaks 1-inch thick
2 tablespoons olive oil or vegetable oil
1 tablespoon steak seasoning or kosher salt & pepper, or to taste
2 tablespoons butter or herbed butter

Directions

  1. Remove steaks from the fridge at least 45 minutes before cooking.
  2. Just before cooking, rub steaks with olive oil and generously season to taste.
  3. Preheat grill to medium heat (approximately 375°F).
  4. Prepare steaks as above and grill 5-6 minutes per side for medium-rare or 6-7 minutes per side for medium.
  5. Remove from the grill and top with a pat of butter, loosely tent with foil. Rest steaks 5-10 minutes before serving.

best meat pizza
pastrami and facon

Serves: 8 Course: Main Type: Meat
Time: 1 Hour Level: Medium Family

Ingredients

2 bread rectangle pizza crusts/flatbreads
2 onions, finely diced
2-3 tbsp oil
2 (6 oz) packages pastrami, thinly sliced
2 (4 oz) packages facon, thinly sliced
3 tbsp brown sugar
6-8 tbsp prepared garlic dip
about ¼ cup Sweet Baby Ray’s BBQ sauce

Directions

  1. Heat oil in a sauté pan over medium heat. Add onions and sauté until nicely browned and caramelized. This can take about 15-20 minutes. Once caramelized, add the thinly sliced pastrami and facon. Mix and sauté another 2-3 minutes. Add brown sugar. Mix and remove from heat. At this point you can also refrigerate or freeze the mixture for later use.
  2. Preheat oven to 350℉. For easier serving, score down the crusts almost through so they will slice easily once done. Another tip for easier serving is to prepare a rectangle made out of heavy duty silver foil for easier transferring.
  3. Place crusts on prepared foil on a baking sheet and smear garlic dip generously over the surface. Top with pastrami mixture. I like to strain the meat mixture in a strainer to remove as much fat as possible, but you don't have to do that, you can simply use a slotted spoon to lift the meat from the pan, leaving the fat behind (the fat in the pan comes from the facon). Drizzle with BBQ sauce. Bake for 20 minutes.
  4. I like to use the plastic containers that come with the pizza bread to store the pizza in the refrigerator until ready to serve.
  5. To serve: Either serve immediately, or, if serving on Shabbos, refrigerate and then bring to room temperature or place over the cholent or hot plate. The meat pizza freezes well, and reheats well. You can also prepare the pastrami mixture in advance and assemble/bake it fresh.

Pepper Steak
with peppers

Serves: 6 Course: Main Type: Meat
Time: 1 hour and 30 Min Level: Easy Family

Ingredients

1 and 1/2 pounds steak
1 onion, sliced
6 cloves garlic
1/2 tablespoon minced ginger
5 peppers in various colors, sliced
1/2 cup red wine
3 teaspoons salt

Directions

  1. Slice steak against the grain into thin strips.
  2. In a pan, heat 1/4 cup oil. Brown meat on each side. Set aside.
  3. In a pan, heat 1/4 cup oil. Brown meat on each side. Set aside.
  4. Using the same pan, sauté onion. Once translucent, add garlic and ginger and cook for two minutes. Add peppers.
  5. In a small bowl, combine wine, coconut aminos, salt, and 1/4 cup oil.
  6. Once the peppers have cooked down, add in the meat and pour on the sauce. Cover and simmer on a low flame for one to two hours (the longer you cook it, the softer it gets).
  7. Serve over rice or quinoa.

savory beef and broccoli
stir fry

Serves: 4 Course: Main Type: Meat
Time: 30 Min Level: Medium Family

Ingredients

1 1/4 lbs flank steak thinly sliced
1 tablespoon + 1 teaspoon vegetable oil divided use
2 cups broccoli florets
2 teaspoons minced fresh ginger
1 teaspoon minced garlic
1/4 cup oyster sauce
1/4 cup beef broth or water
1 teaspoon sugar
2 teaspoons toasted sesame oil
1 teaspoon soy sauce
1 teaspoon cornstarch
salt and pepper to taste

Directions

  1. Heat 1 teaspoon of oil in a large frying pan over medium heat. Add the broccoli and cook for approximately 4 minutes or until tender.
  2. Add the ginger and garlic to the pan and cook for 30 seconds more.
  3. Remove the broccoli from the pan; place on a plate and cover.
  4. Wipe the pan clean with a paper towel and turn the heat to high. Add the remaining tablespoon of oil.
  5. Season the steak pieces with salt and pepper and add them to the pan in a single layer - you may need to do this step in batches. Cook for 3-4 minutes on each side until browned and cooked through.
  6. Add the broccoli mixture back to the pan and cook for 2 more minutes or until warmed through.
  7. In a bowl whisk together the oyster sauce, beef broth, sugar, sesame oil and soy sauce. In a small bowl mix the cornstarch with a tablespoon of cold water.
  8. Pour the oyster sauce mixture over the beef and vegetables; cook for 30 seconds. Add the cornstarch and bring to a boil; cook for 1 more minute or until sauce has just started to thicken.
  9. Serve immediately, with rice if desired.

Top meat knives
Butcher Knife:
Features a broad, heavy, often curved blade for rocking through thick raw meat and joints, ideal for breaking down large primal cuts.		 Heavy Chef's Knife:
Its strong heel can be used to power through dense parts, though it's less specialized than a cleaver or butcher knife.
Boning Knife:
A strong, rigid blade designed for the laborious task of separating meat from bone and trimming sinew. 		Carving Knife:
While great for cooked roasts, its sharp, pointed tip can help navigate around bones in raw meat too.
Meat Cleaver:
A weighty, rectangular blade built to chop through bone and dense connective tissue, perfect for things like spareribs or joints.		 Cimiter Knife:
A long, curved blade that pulls itself through meat for single-stroke cuts, excellent for large roasts and briskets.


Prepare and Cook Meats
Dry and Temper:
Pat the meat thoroughly dry with paper towels to ensure a crisp sear, and let it sit at room temperature for 30 minutes to promote even cooking.		 Avoid Washing:
Never rinse raw meat; this does not kill bacteria and instead spreads pathogens across your sink and countertops. [2, 3]
Match Method to Cut:
Use high, dry heat (searing/grilling) for tender cuts like steaks, and low, slow, moist heat (braising) for tough cuts like brisket or chuck. 		Use a Thermometer:
Ignore "color" or "feel" and use a food thermometer to hit safe internal targets: 160°F for ground meat, and 165°F for poultry.
Don’t Overcrowd:
Leave space between pieces in the pan so the meat sears and browns properly rather than steaming in its own released moisture. 		Rest Before Slicing:
Let the meat rest for 5–10 minutes after cooking to allow juices to redistribute, ensuring the meat stays moist when cut.
